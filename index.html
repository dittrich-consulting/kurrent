How practice and scoring work

Adaptive practice gives untried and low-scoring letters more weight, with a smaller recency adjustment. Random mode gives each enabled letter equal probability. Frequency mode uses approximate German letter weights (an approximate practice distribution, not a corpus measurement), with a small minimum chance for rare letters and capitals sharing their lowercase weights.

Both shapes are thinned to centerlines and normalized to a common size. A bounded affine search aligns them with up to 25° rotation, moderate shear, and proportion adjustments. A symmetric distance compares all strokes in both directions, including missing dots and extra strokes. The score is a fixed heuristic from 0 to 10, not a validated handwriting grade. A familiar error does not earn a higher score; your history is used for feedback and exercise selection.

Size is recorded relative to the displayed exemplar; original size and position are ignored for scoring. Stroke thickness is normalized rather than graded. Rotation and skew are approximate and can be ambiguous for simple shapes. The font is the visual scoring reference.
